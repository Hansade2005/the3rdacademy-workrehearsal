# Engineering Doctrine — D5

These are the non-negotiable rules for the D5 build. They override personal preference, prior convention, and downstream convenience. Where a rule is shared with D1, it is unchanged. Where D5 differs, the difference is named explicitly.

### 1. D5 centre of gravity

D5 is about the working space between two people when disagreement enters it. D1 asked whether truth shows up under pressure. D2 asked whether repair shows up after a slip. D3 asked whether others can build plans around your pattern. D4 asked what your words made another person carry. D5 asks whether your behaviour keeps progress possible when agreement is no longer easy.

> ⚙️  **HANS NOTE — The participant must feel**
> 
> "The work did not fail because people were incompetent. It failed because the working relationship carrying the work became damaged."

### 2. The Relational Pressure Chain

Every major D5 moment must show this chain:

> *Different positions → hidden interests → relationship move → field shift → other person’s next move → future collaboration cost.*

A scene that only asks "What is the better teamwork response?" is ordinary training. A scene that asks "What did your move do to the working space between you?" is T3A.

### 3. The Four-Question D5 Production Rule

Every D5 scene must answer these four questions:

- What are both people trying to protect?

- What disagreement is visible?

- What disagreement is hidden?

- What did the participant’s move do to the working relationship?

If a scene cannot answer all four, it does not belong in D5.

### 4. Engine and content are separate

No D5 content is hard-coded into engine logic. All decision paths, consequence horizons, narration blocks, artifact prompts, and panel copy live in the Content Registry. The engine renders schema fields. The script populates them.

### 5. Developer-only visibility rule

Observable Indicator codes (OI-1 through OI-8) and Failure Mode codes (FM-1 through FM-7) must never appear in any participant-facing surface. Not in Behaviour Signal Panels, not in the Collaboration Pattern Mirror, not in the Growth Log, not in the Retention Check. Participant copy uses plain-language signal phrasing only ("What this may signal: …"). Codes are present in this document as developer reference inside square-bracket schema tags.

### 6. The Path A Cost Rule

The most mature path in every scenario must carry an immediate visible cost. The participant must feel that the relationally intelligent move costs something now and pays later. No frictionless mature paths.

> ⚙️  **HANS NOTE — Why "mature" and not "ethical" in D5**
> 
> In D1–D3 the right move was often the ethical move (tell the truth, repair the slip, keep the pattern). In D5 the best move is rarely a moral choice between right and wrong. It is a relationally intelligent move under workplace pressure — hear the other position, name the shared interest, build the bridge. The cost rule still applies: the mature path must carry an immediate visible cost.

### 7. Rehearsal, not evaluation

D5 is a behavioural rehearsal experience. The platform does not certify, validate, prove, screen, endorse, score, or assess. Participant copy never uses these terms. The product is a private rehearsal mirror.

### 8. Recognition before naming

The signature framework (The Alignment Method) is named in Segment C, not in Segment A. The participant must first feel the pressure, then recognise the pattern, then receive the name.

### 9. The Alignment Method (signature framework)

Final participant-facing version:

> *Hear the Position → Name the Shared Interest → Build the Bridge → Protect the Working Bond*

Named only at Brief C3. Returned at Screen G-1.5. Referenced by name in Debrief D-8 and SC4 Path B.

### 10. D5 signature mechanics (four)

- Dual-Perspective Replay — the same conflict heard from two chairs (Segment D).

- Relationship Field View — five states (open / guarded / transactional / avoidant / adversarial), rendered as a private mirror, never as a score.

- Role Reversal Artifact — the participant writes from the other person’s position (SC3 minimum, returns in Segment G).

- The Third Object — the conflict is moved from "you versus me" toward the shared problem both people must face. Visual: two figures turn from each other toward the shared object between them.

### 11. Audio discipline

The Tibetan singing bowl (locked T3A brand asset, approximately 528 Hz) appears only at consequence-horizon transitions inside Segment E. The four-note motif appears at A-0 (cinematic entry), at the Growth Log header inside Segment G, and at the awe close. No other audio cues use either signature.

### 12. Cinematic entry

Segment A opens with cinematic pressure, not orientation. The participant is dropped into a collaboration fracture before any framework is introduced.

### 13. Three-horizon consequence threading

Every Scenario Lab path renders three consequence horizons: Same Day, Next Week, Month End. Tibetan singing bowl plays at each horizon transition.

### 14. Artifact-write with reference calibration

Every scenario requires the participant to write a real workplace artifact (message, note, document edit, comment). Reference calibration appears only after submission. The participant never sees the calibration before writing.

### 15. Cross-module memory

D5 consumes D4 memory (impact-transfer state and final personal sentence) and emits D5 memory for D6 (final personal sentence + final field state + final pattern mirror). Documented in the Cross-Module Memory Contract section and in the Schema Field Map.

### 16. The Retention Check is not a quiz

The Retention Check is a re-rehearsal. The participant sees one of their own previous responses 3–7 days later and answers one question: "Is this still how I would handle the space between us?" No grades, no pass/fail, no mastery language.

### 17. No call-to-action inside Segment G

The awe close is quiet. No upsell to observation pathway, no marketing for other modules, no "what’s next" callout. The participant’s last contact with D5 is the personal sentence they wrote, not a sales surface.

### 18. Banned terms (participant-facing)

- validate, certify, assess, prove, screen, endorse, score

- teach (as a BridgeFast claim)

- mastery, mastery check, pass, fail, grade

- compromise (as the goal of conflict)

- learner — use "participant"

### 19. Locked vocabulary

- "under workplace pressure" — not "under real pressure" or "under real-world pressure"

- "Section" — not "§"

- Decimals less than 1 carry a leading zero (0.42, not .42)

- No abbreviations of product or proper nouns: "Probation Blueprint" not "PB"; "The 3rd Academy" not "T3A" in any participant-facing copy.

- V4: A senior leader’s LinkedIn post tags you for analytical work that was largely a peer’s. The post has 184 likes. Your peer has not liked the post.

## SC3 Retention Variants — Style Clash family

- V1: A colleague’s frequent re-prioritising frustrates you. You almost message a third party about it. You have started typing the message twice and deleted it twice.

- V2: A colleague’s slow methodical pace is delaying a deliverable you own. Their Slack status reads "deep work — do not disturb." It has read that for two days.

- V3: A colleague’s "let’s discuss it" approach is interfering with your "let’s decide and move" approach. They have just sent a calendar invite titled "alignment chat — 30 mins" for tomorrow.

- V4: A colleague’s preference for written async is interfering with your preference for live sync. They have replied to your meeting request with a five-paragraph document instead.
