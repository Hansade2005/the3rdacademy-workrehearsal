# Engineering Doctrine — D8

These are the non-negotiable rules for the D8 build. They override personal preference, prior convention, and downstream convenience.

### 1. D8 centre of gravity

D8 is about the period after a setback, overload, embarrassment, visible mistake, disruption, or pressure event — and whether the participant’s recovery behaviour restores trust or creates new damage. D1 asked whether truth shows up under pressure. D2 asked whether repair shows up after a slip. D3 asked whether others can build plans around your pattern. D4 asked what your words made another person carry. D5 asked whether your behaviour keeps progress possible when agreement is no longer easy. D8 asks whether your recovery makes the next workplace moment safer or harder for the people working around you.

> ⚙️  **HANS NOTE — The participant must feel**
> 
> "My recovery is something other people have to work with."

### 2. THE CLINICAL BOUNDARY — highest IP and regulatory risk

D8 carries higher regulatory exposure than any prior BridgeFast module. The territory — setback, recovery, capacity, masking — sits adjacent to clinical psychology and occupational mental health. A careless render of a single clinical term moves T3A into licensed-practitioner territory in multiple jurisdictions.

> ⚙️  **HANS NOTE — Hard banned terms (D8-specific, extending the universal banned list)**
> 
> NEVER appear in any participant-facing surface:
> 
> • trauma response • anxiety response • burnout signal • depression
> 
> • dysregulation • diagnosis • mental health flag • psychological profile
> 
> Also avoid as primary D8 framing:
> 
> • healing • therapy • emotional regulation (as clinical language) • coping skills (as main frame)
> 
> • grit • toughness • mental strength • bounce back
> 
> D8 stays in workplace-behavioural language only. "Recovery pattern" not "trauma response." "Stabilising" not "regulating." "Masking" is allowed as a workplace-behavioural label only — not as a clinical diagnosis.

### 3. The Recovery Pressure Chain

Every major D8 moment must show this chain:

> *Pressure hit → internal state → recovery move → visible behaviour → trust shift → future capacity assigned.*

A scene that only asks "What is the better recovery response?" is ordinary training. A scene that asks "What did your recovery move make others trust or doubt about working with you?" is T3A.

### 4. The Eight-Question D8 Production Rule

Every D8 scene must answer these eight questions:

- What hit the participant?

- What is still unstable?

- What does the participant want others to believe?

- What does the work actually need?

- What recovery move does the participant choose?

- What does that move make others trust or doubt?

- What happens one week later?

- What recovery pattern is visible one month later?

If a scene cannot answer all eight, it does not belong in D8.

### 5. Engine and content are separate

No D8 content is hard-coded into engine logic. All decision paths, consequence horizons, narration blocks, artifact prompts, and panel copy live in the Content Registry. The engine renders schema fields. The script populates them.

### 6. Developer-only visibility rule

Observable Indicator codes (OI-1 through OI-8) and Failure Mode codes (FM-1 through FM-7) must never appear in any participant-facing surface. Participant copy uses plain-language signal phrasing only ("What this may signal: …"). Codes are present in this document as developer reference inside square-bracket schema tags.

### 7. The Path A Cost Rule

The most mature path in every scenario must carry an immediate visible cost. The participant must feel that the relationally intelligent recovery move costs something now and pays later. No frictionless mature paths.

### 8. Rehearsal, not evaluation

D8 is a behavioural rehearsal experience. The platform does not certify, validate, prove, screen, endorse, score, or assess. D8 explicitly does not diagnose, identify, label, or flag mental health states. The product is a private rehearsal mirror.

### 9. Recognition before naming

The signature framework (The Recovery Method) is named only at Brief C3. Returned at Screen G-1.5. Referenced by name in Debrief D-9 and SC4 Path B.

### 10. The Recovery Method (signature framework)

Final participant-facing version:

> *Name → Stabilise → Re-enter → Restore*

Named only at Brief C3. Returned at G-1.5.

### 11. D8 signature mechanics (six)

- Recovery Pattern Mirror — cumulative mirror after every scenario. Columns: Scenario / Pressure Hit / Recovery Move / Trust After Recovery.

- The Second Hit — a second pressure arrives before recovery is complete. Required in SC2.

- Masking Detector — behavioural mirror (NOT diagnostic) with five allowed workplace-behavioural labels. Required in SC3.

- Return-to-Trust Moment — structured five-part statement (Here is what happened / what I have stabilised / what I can own now / where I need adjustment / when I will close the loop). Required in SC4 and returns in Segment G.

- Pre-Trust Replay — before the SC4 decision, show what the manager already believes about the participant’s recovery pattern. The participant decides inside an existing trust field, not in a vacuum.

- Recovery Retrospective — in Audio Case Beat D-8, the participant retrospectively answers what should have been said or done earlier. Recovery failure often begins earlier than the visible collapse.

### 12. Masking Detector labels (locked)

> ⚙️  **HANS NOTE — Allowed participant-facing labels (five only)**
> 
> Over-assuring — saying "I’ve got it" without evidence or adjustment.
> 
> Silent carrying — taking on more while capacity is already affected.
> 
> Defensive normal — acting normal while becoming sharper, slower, or avoidant.
> 
> Controlled disclosure — naming impact clearly without transferring emotional burden.
> 
> Recovery with ownership — asking for adjustment while still owning next steps.

> ⚠️  **DEVELOPER WARNING — Developer warning — prohibited labels**
> 
> The Masking Detector must never use clinical or diagnostic labels. The five allowed labels above are the entire vocabulary. No "anxiety response." No "trauma reaction." No "burnout indicator." No "mental health flag." A careless extension of the label set is a regulatory breach.

### 15. Three-horizon consequence threading

### 19. No call-to-action inside Segment G

# What Makes This Module Different

D8 is not resilience content. It is recovery-pattern rehearsal. It shows what others experience from the participant after a setback, and whether recovery behaviour restores trust or creates new damage.

Five structural separations from ordinary corporate resilience training:

- The Recovery Pattern Mirror tracks behaviour across four scenarios as a cumulative pattern, not a single moment.

- The Second Hit (SC2) explicitly rehearses what happens when a new demand arrives before recovery is complete — the moment most resilience courses never name.

- The Masking Detector (SC3) is a workplace-behavioural mirror with five locked labels and an anti-diagnostic footer. It is not a clinical instrument.

- The Pre-Trust Replay (before SC4) renders what the manager already believes about the participant’s recovery pattern BEFORE the participant decides. The participant enters the conversation inside an existing trust field, not a vacuum. This is the category-of-one architectural mechanic across all six BridgeFast modules.

- The Recovery Retrospective (Beat D-8) asks the participant to locate the earlier moment — before the visible collapse — where recovery could have stabilised. This converts hindsight into rehearsal.

What D8 explicitly refuses to be:

- Not stress-management tips.

- Not mental health advice.

- Not positive thinking, toughness, grit, or "bounce back" language.

- Not a burnout diagnosis tool.

- Not an emotional disclosure forum.

D8 does not ask whether you were hit. It asks what your recovery made possible — or harder — for the people working around you.

> ***We do not teach resilience as a mindset. We rehearse recovery as workplace behaviour.***
