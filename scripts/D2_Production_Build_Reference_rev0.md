This is not a script for a learning module. Each visual element, audio cue, narration line, timing window, and decision option in this script has been argued, destruction-tested, and approved against the Dimension Production Standard rev0. Casual edits to any of these break product integrity.

**5. What the participant feels**

If a module is working as designed, the participant should feel:

• “Wait, this is not what I expected.”

• “This isn’t asking me to remember things — it’s asking me what I would actually do.”

• “This system seems to know what I said last time.”

• “This is making me face the actual moments where ownership shows.”

That is the experience T3A is aiming for. Any other inner monologue — “well-produced content,” “polished module,” “clever interaction” — and the architecture is not landing.

**6. The Statement**

That is why the wording matters. The 3rd Academy is not differentiated by simulation alone. It is differentiated by a distinct behavioural architecture that gives participants a different experience of workplace readiness. D2 is the moment that architecture proves it scales beyond one module.

### Screen B-1 — What ownership actually looks like at work
| **💻 ON-SCREEN:** **Ownership is not the same as authorship.** Authorship is who wrote the code, who scoped the project, who drafted the document. Ownership is who carries the outcome when something goes well — and when something goes wrong. The two are often the same person, but not always. Knowing the difference is the start of the dimension. At work, ownership shows in six places. You will recognise these from your own teams: • The first thirty seconds after something slips. Owners flag early. Non-owners flag late or not at all. • The framing of the slip. Owners describe what happened. Non-owners describe what happened to them. • The repair offer. Owners propose a specific next step. **5. Cross-scenario threading — D2 dimension-specific signature [declared]**

**Threading state model — repair posture. **After each scenario decision, the engine writes a repair_posture value to the Mirror state. The aggregate posture across prior scenarios is summarised as one of three context variants (complete / partial / absent):

• complete — every prior scenario showed the full Repair Sequence (posture = COMPLETE across the board).

• partial — prior scenarios show a mix of COMPLETE and PARTIAL postures; the repair pattern is uneven.

• absent — majority of prior scenarios were PARTIAL or ABSENT; the repair sequence is breaking down.

# What Makes This Module Different (Marketing Layer)
*Extracted at deployment per §1.10. This section is the participant-facing positioning for D2 — what gets surfaced on landing pages, in employer touch-1 letters, and in product descriptions.*

D2 is behavioural rehearsal under workplace pressure, for the moments where accountability quietly breaks down.

### The premise
Most accountability failures at work do not come from bad people. They come from decent people in workplace pressure moments making small adjustments that feel reasonable in the moment and that quietly cost trust over time. D2 puts you inside five such moments and asks you to choose. Then it shows you what your choice produced — not in seconds, but across the same day, the next week, and the month end.

### What you carry out
One four-step framework you can use the next time something you committed to slips. It does not come from a book. It comes from the moments you just lived in this module. You name it after you have already used it.

### How D2 is different
Most workplace tools explain accountability. D2 makes you rehearse the moments where accountability breaks. You choose a path, write the actual message you would send, and see how that choice plays out across the same day, the next week, and the month end. No ranking. No public result. No shared record. Just private calibration.

### Who D2 is for
Software engineers, technical leads, and adjacent roles in their first 90 days, returning to behavioural calibration, or crossing a national border to take up a role in a new country. D2 ships at MVP for the Technology/Software sector with overlays for Cross-Border Professionals and Experienced Workers.

### What carries forward
D2 reads the sentence you wrote at the end of D1 and reflects it back to you in the first minute. The sentence you write at the end of D2 becomes the opening of D3. The behavioural rehearsal record persists across modules. The system remembers what you brought.

### Retention check architecture (Re-Rehearsal mode)
3–7 days post-module-completion, the engine schedules a notification (mobile push + email). On tap, the participant returns to one of the four Scenario Chains they rehearsed in D2. The scenario re-renders with their submitted artifact-write displayed verbatim. One question follows.

### Scenario selection logic
Engine selects 1 of SC1–SC4 weighted by participant deliberation_time on the original chain (higher deliberation = stronger callback signal). Random tiebreak. Never re-selects the same scenario within 30 days on a return visit.

### The single retention question (the only thing the participant sees)
“You rehearsed this scenario three to seven days ago. Here is the message you wrote at the time: [participant’s verbatim sc{N}_artifact_write_text]. Reading it now, is this still what you would do?”

### Two-option response
• “Yes, this is still what I would do.” — captured as retention_alignment = high.

• “I would write it differently now.” — opens a brief text input: “In one sentence, what would you change?” Captured as retention_alignment = revised + retention_revision_text.

### Internal implementation note — no public ranking, no completion gate, no return loop
The retention check has no public ranking, no qualifying outcome, and no completion-gate language. It only records whether the participant's judgment pattern remained stable after time passed. The Completion Record reflects completion only.
