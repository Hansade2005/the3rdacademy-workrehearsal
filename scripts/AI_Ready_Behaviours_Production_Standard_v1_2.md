**Market Position and Moat-Critical Features**

**The shelf we are entering**

The market for AI-at-work training is filling quickly with policy explainers, prompt-engineering courses, and "responsible AI" modules. Almost all of them teach what to do. They tell a participant to verify output, to disclose AI use, to escalate when something looks wrong. Telling is not rehearsal. None of the reviewed products place a participant inside the pressure moment and observe the behaviour that actually shows up. AI-Ready Behaviours enters the shelf as rehearsal under workplace pressure, not instruction.

The moat-critical architecture that places The 3rd Academy in a category competitors are not in is specified in the Dimension Production Standard, Section 1.4.A through Section 1.4.C, and carries into every AI-Ready Behaviours module unchanged: cinematic discipline, three-horizon consequence threading, cross-module memory, two-axis variant calibration, signature audio, and the Growth Log with the Lock 2 behavioural fingerprint. This standard does not re-derive them. It adds five differentiators specific to AI-judgment rehearsal: the Inspect Surface, within-module memory, the Recognition Moment, voice integrity, and the Mirror Rule.

### Section 1.1.A — Retention Check Structural Specification (new in v1.1)

The Retention Check fires 3 to 7 days after module completion, through the participant's preferred channel (in-app, email, or SMS). Its purpose is to capture whether the central behavioural lesson of the module survived the working week between completion and check-in. The Retention Check is the participant's second moment with the module's content — it cannot be a quiz, a survey, or a re-run.

***Required structure***

- **One scenario fragment, not a question.** A 30 to 45-second micro-scene set in the same fictional workplace as the module, presenting a smaller version of the pressure point. The participant chooses one of three responses.

- **Cross-module memory active.** If the participant's behaviour in the original module produced an emission contract, the Retention Check fragment incorporates it (Section 1.4.A).

- **One Same-Day-only horizon.** To respect the 3 to 7-day cadence, the Retention Check renders only the Same-Day consequence horizon. The Next-Week and Month-End horizons remain locked to the original module.

- **No score, no "correct answer" overlay.** The Retention Check inherits the no-grading rule of the Inspect Surface (Section 1.4.D). The participant sees the Same-Day consequence and is returned to the Growth Log.

- **Lock 2 capture, never displayed.** The Retention Check captures one signal — whether the participant's choice matches, drifts from, or reverses their choice in the original module — and persists it as a Lock 2 fingerprint signal. Never surfaced to the participant.

- **Tibetan-bell anchored.** Same signature audio as the original module's time-jump transitions. The participant recognises the lab corridor instantly.

## Section 1.4 — Engine and Content Separation

The one-engine principle is specified in full in the Dimension Production Standard, Section 1.4, and governs AI-Ready Behaviours unchanged: **one dimension-blind engine, many modules; content is data, not code; new modules load without engine changes.** AI-Ready modules run on the same engine as Probation Blueprint. The layered scenario, however, asks the engine for behaviours the 90-minute format never required. Per the engine-separation rule, these are added once as versioned schema extensions that all future modules can use — never as a per-module workaround.

**Required engine extensions for AI-Ready Behaviours** (added through the versioned-extension process before the suite ships):

- **Second decision point per scenario.** A scenario.pressure_beat extension that lets one scenario carry a second decision (three paths) whose result composes with the first to select the consequence horizon set. The engine stays dimension-blind; it simply renders a second decision when the field is present.

- **Inspect Surface. [MOAT-CRITICAL]** A scenario.inspect_artifact extension that renders the source material and the AI output on one surface and captures the participant's interaction with it (highlight, select-supported-claims, or annotate). Specified in Section 1.4.D.

- **Within-module memory extension (new in v1.1).** A module.intra_module_state extension that lets LS2 read the participant's LS1 decisions and consequence path, and adjusts LS2's Inspect Surface and Decide prompts accordingly. Specified in Section 1.4.A.1.

***Section 1.4.A — Cross-Module and Cross-Lab Memory [MOAT-CRITICAL]***

The cross-module memory engine specification is in the Dimension Production Standard, Section 1.4.A, and applies unchanged. **AI-Ready adds two capabilities: cross-lab reads, and within-module memory (new in v1.1).** An AI-Ready module may read the participant's emission contracts from BehaviourLab modules as well as from prior AI-Ready modules. The clearest use: the AI Disclosure & Attribution cold open can reference the participant's D1 (Integrity & Ethics) final personal sentence verbatim — so a participant who has rehearsed honesty in BehaviourLab hears their own words when the AI-assisted honesty test arrives. Forward references remain blocked; the engine reads only modules completed before the current load.

***Section 1.4.A.1 — Within-Module Memory [MOAT-CRITICAL] (new in v1.1)***

LS1's outcome subtly threads into LS2 inside the same module. The mechanism is the engine's intra_module_state extension; the doctrine is that the participant feels the through-line of their behaviour across both scenarios in a single 45-minute experience.

**Required threads (a module must implement at least two of the four):**

- **Inspect Surface residue.** LS2's source-vs-output panels include a small element that references LS1's outcome — for example, a Slack message in LS2's source panel quotes a phrase the participant produced or accepted in LS1.

- **Decide prompt drift.** LS2's Decide-beat prompt acknowledges the participant's LS1 path. If the participant held their call under LS1 pressure, LS2's Decide framing differs subtly from the framing offered to a participant who folded.

- **Cross-character reference.** A character who appeared in LS1 reappears in LS2 with a tonal shift that reflects what the participant did. Never accusatory; never explanatory.

- **Artifact carry-over.** The artifact the participant wrote or sent in LS1 appears, edited or referenced, inside LS2's Inspect Surface.

***Section 1.4.D — The Inspect Surface (Engine Specification) [MOAT-CRITICAL]***

The Inspect Surface is the engine component that renders the opening beat of every layered scenario. It is the AI-Ready counterpart to the cold open's pressure entry, applied to evidence.

**Engine behaviour.** The engine reads scenario.inspect_artifact and renders two panels on one surface: a source panel (the underlying data, brief, or transcript) and an output panel (the AI-generated artifact). The content layer declares the panels and the interaction type. The engine captures the participant's interaction as a Growth Log event and — internally only — as a Lock 2 signal. It does not score the interaction or display a verdict.

**Input — schema fields the engine reads:** inspect_artifact.source_panel (text or structured data), inspect_artifact.output_panel (the AI artifact), inspect_artifact.interaction_type (one of: highlight_unsupported, select_supported_claims, annotate), inspect_artifact.gap_anchors[] (developer-only markers of where source and output diverge, used for Lock 2 capture, never shown), inspect_artifact.mobile_mode (one of: stacked_swipe, collapsible, comparison_tap — required, new in v1.1).

**Output:** a rendered side-by-side surface (desktop) or mobile-equivalent (Section 1.4.D mobile rules below); a captured interaction persisted to the Growth Log; a Lock 2 signal recording how thoroughly the participant examined the gap. The participant sees no score and no "correct answer" overlay.

***Mobile-first Inspect Surface rules (new in v1.1)***

The Inspect Surface is moat-critical. The phone is where most participants — newcomers, settlement-program learners, internationally-trained workers — will use the platform. Side-by-side panels on a small screen either become unreadable or stack unconsidered. The Inspect Surface must therefore declare a mobile mode and pass mobile usability before ship.

**Required mobile modes (the content layer must declare one per Inspect Surface):**

- **Stacked-Swipe.** Source panel above, output panel below. Participant swipes between them. Best for short artifacts (Slack threads, short emails).

- **Collapsible.** Both panels visible, each collapsed by default. Participant taps to expand either. Best for long artifacts where focus matters (data tables, reports).

- **Comparison-Tap.** Source panel only by default; tapping a claim in the output panel surfaces the matched (or absent) source evidence as an inline overlay. Best for artifacts with many small claims (AI summaries, dashboards).

***Section 1.4.D.1 — Growth Log Render Rules for Inspect Interactions (new in v1.1)***

The Inspect Surface captures the participant's interaction as a Growth Log event. What the participant later sees in their Growth Log must protect the no-grading rule. - **No reference to gap correctness.** The Growth Log does not state whether the participant found the gap. The consequence horizons in the module did that work; the Growth Log only records that the work happened.

## Section 2.4 — The Inspect Beat: Source-Artifact Requirements [MOAT-CRITICAL]

This is the depth guardrail. It is what makes a two-scenario, 45-minute module deeper than a four-scenario one, rather than thinner.

- **Mandatory.** Every layered scenario opens with an Inspect beat. A scenario without one does not ship.

- **Real divergence.** The source panel and the output panel must genuinely diverge — an overstated certainty, an unsupported figure, a concealed assumption. The gap must be discoverable by a careful reader, not invented or trivial.

- **Legible without grading.** The participant must be able to see the gap through the layout alone. The engine never marks it correct or incorrect (Section 1.4.D).

- **Carried into consequence.** Whether the participant caught the gap must matter to the consequence horizons. An Inspect beat with no downstream consequence is decoration and fails the Depth Gate (Section 3.8).

***Artifact Craft Standard per medium (new in v1.1)***

The Inspect Surface only delivers "wow" when the artifact itself feels real. "Real divergence" is necessary but not sufficient. The artifact must read as something the participant could have received yesterday from a colleague.

**Per-medium realism criteria.** The producer declares the medium of each Inspect artifact and meets its realism floor:

| **Medium**                      | **Minimum realism criteria**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

| **Slack / chat thread**         | Time stamps in the participant's plausible workday; realistic emoji / reaction usage (or its absence); message lengths that vary as real conversations do; at least one moment of mid-thread editing or correction visible; channel name plausible (#design-review, \#q3-launch, not \#channel).           |

### Section 2.4.1 — The Recognition Moment Mandate [MOAT-CRITICAL] (new in v1.1)

Every layered scenario must contain at least one Recognition Moment: a micro-detail that a real practitioner in the target audience would immediately identify as exactly how it happens in their workplace. The Recognition Moment is what converts a competent simulation into recognised reality — the difference between "this is plausible" and "this is my Tuesday."

**Required characteristics:**

- **Specific, not generic.** A Recognition Moment is never "there is time pressure." It is "the message comes in at 4:47 PM on a Friday with a one-word subject line and three exclamation marks."

- **Drawn from real practice.** The producer must be able to point to the source — an interview, a personal observation, a documented case — where the detail was first observed. Manufactured-feeling details do not qualify.

- **Calibrated to the audience-context variant.** A Recognition Moment for a New Hire variant differs from one for an Experienced Worker or Cross-Border Professional variant. A new hire recognises the particular way a manager phrases a polite no; an experienced worker recognises the political shape of a stalled approval chain; a cross-border professional recognises the specific moment when their direct phrasing is taken as rude.

- **Declared in the build worksheet.** Each Recognition Moment is named explicitly in the build worksheet (Appendix B) — the producer cannot claim "it's woven throughout." If it cannot be pointed to, it is not there.

- **Different from the Mirror Rule.** The Recognition Moment is environmental: it makes the workplace feel real. The Mirror Rule (Section 3.8) is personal: it makes the participant see themselves. Both must be present; they are not the same mechanism.

## Section 2.5 — The Pressure Beat: Escalation Requirements

The Pressure beat is the second half of the layered scenario's depth. After the Decide beat, the situation escalates and the participant decides again.

- **Genuine escalation.** New information, a deadline, or a person pushing back — something that tests whether the first decision holds. "Are you sure?" is not escalation; a manager restating the original, riskier framing and asking the participant to ship it is.

- **Both decisions compose.** The consequence set is selected from the combination of the Decide and Pressure decisions, not the Pressure decision alone. Holding a good call under pressure, and folding a good call under pressure, must lead to visibly different horizons.

***Pressure-Beat Type Diversity Rule (new in v1.1)***

A two-scenario module that uses the same pressure type in LS1 and LS2 leaves a third of its behavioural surface unobserved. Pressure types are not interchangeable; people who fold under authority pressure may hold under epistemic pressure, and vice versa. Each module must observe both.

**The five locked pressure types:**

| **Pressure type**      | **What it tests**                                                            | **Example trigger**                                                                                                |
|------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Authority**          | Whether the call survives instruction from a higher-status actor             | A manager re-states the original framing and asks the participant to ship it.                                      |
| **Social / peer**      | Whether the call survives the cost of disagreeing with a peer or team        | A colleague the participant respects says "we always do it this way" and adds an emoji.                            |
| **Epistemic**          | Whether the call survives new information that complicates it                | A second source surfaces that partially supports the original AI output.                                           |
| **Time / operational** | Whether the call survives a shortened window                                 | The deadline moves from end of day to end of hour; the inbox is filling.                                           |
| **Policy ambiguity**   | Whether the call survives a vague rule the participant could read either way | The internal AI-use policy says "disclose materially." The participant must decide whether their use was material. |

## Section 2.9 — Voice Integrity [MOAT-CRITICAL] (new in v1.1)

The written voice of an AI-Ready Behaviours module is itself a moat-critical element. The cinematic discipline of the cold open and the awe-moment close depends on language that does not announce itself; the no-grading rule of the Inspect Surface depends on prompts that observe rather than instruct; the felt quality of the within-module thread depends on microcopy that names what is happening without explaining why. Two producers writing for two modules will produce two different voices unless the voice is locked.

**The locked AI-Ready voice:**

| **Voice principle**       | **What it means in practice**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Quiet**                 | Sentences are short and unadorned. No exclamation marks. No "powerful" verbs. The reader supplies the energy; the prose does not push it onto them.                                                                                                                  |
| **Observational**         | Narrations name what is happening, not what it means. "The message arrives at 4:47 PM" — never "The message arrives at 4:47 PM, increasing the pressure."                                                                                                            |
| **Present-tense**         | Scenario language is present-tense throughout. Consequence horizons remain present-tense even at Next Week and Month End: "It is Friday. The reviewer's reply is in your inbox."                                                                                     |
| **Second-person sparing** | "You" is used where the participant is acting; replaced with the workplace name ("the inbox", "the dashboard") wherever the camera could carry the meaning instead.                                                                                                  |
| **No instruction**        | The voice never tells the participant what to do or notice. The Inspect Surface is laid out; the participant decides what to look at. Microcopy reads "the source is on the left; the output is on the right," never "check whether the source supports the output." |
| **No editorial on AI**    | The voice never celebrates AI, never fear-mongers about AI, never anthropomorphises AI. AI appears as a fact of the workplace, like email.                                                                                                                           |
| **No cleverness**         | Wit, irony, and meta-commentary destroy the cinematic register. The voice trusts the situation to be interesting and stays out of its way.                                                                                                                           |

## Section 3.8 — The Depth Gate [MOAT-CRITICAL] (expanded in v1.1)

The AI-Ready-only depth gate. It exists because the two-scenario core is justified entirely by depth; if depth is not present, the module is simply a thinner version of a four-scenario module, and the adaptation has failed. A module cannot ship unless a reviewer confirms all five:

- **Depth Gate Check 1.** The Inspect beat shows a real, discoverable evidence gap (Section 2.4) — not a trivial or invented one.

- **Depth Gate Check 2.** Whether the participant caught the gap changes the consequence horizons — inspection is consequential, not decorative.

- **Depth Gate Check 3.** The Pressure beat is a genuine second decision under escalation (Section 2.5) — not a reflection prompt — and uses a different pressure type than the partner scenario in the module.

- **Depth Gate Check 4.** Holding versus folding under pressure leads to visibly different horizons — the two decisions compose.

- **Depth Gate Check 5 — The Mirror Rule (new in v1.1).** Every module must contain at least one moment that produces a self-recognition statement from at least one participant in any small pilot batch: "that is what I do." Self-recognition is the architecture's deepest signal. A module that does not produce it in pilot is not yet rehearsal — it is simulation.

## Section 3.9 — The Usability Gate (new in v1.1)

Depth without usability is theatre that no one stays for. A module can pass the Depth Gate and still feel slow, cluttered, cognitively heavy, or unworkable on a phone. The Usability Gate exists so a module is rejected for being unpleasant to experience — independent of whether its behavioural depth is correct.

**Usability Gate checks (all five required to pass):**

| **Check**                      | **Standard**                                                                                                                                                                |
|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **U1. Pacing**                 | No segment exceeds its tolerance band (Section 1.2). No within-segment beat sits idle for more than 12 seconds without participant input or visible motion.                 |
| **U2. Cognitive load**         | Each Inspect Surface presents one comparison at a time. No screen requires the participant to hold more than four pieces of information in working memory simultaneously.   |
| **U3. Mobile pass**            | The module is completed end-to-end on a phone screen ≤ 6.1" by at least one tester. The Inspect Surface's declared mobile mode delivers a discoverable gap (Section 1.4.D). |
| **U4. Clarity of next action** | At every decision point, the three paths are mutually distinct and each is described in 12 words or fewer. No ambiguous "both A and B" options.                             |
| **U5. Smooth interaction**     | No interaction takes more than 1.5 seconds to register. No avatar gesture or audio sting interrupts an interaction the participant has initiated.                           |

## Section 3.10 — The Pilot Evidence Gate (new in v1.1)

The Strategic Partner Review (Section 3.4) tests the module against the Standard. The Pilot Evidence Gate tests the module against real participants. A module that passes every internal gate but produces no felt reaction in pilot is not ready to ship — it has met the Standard but not the experience target.

**Pilot scope:** at least six participants from the target audience, drawn from at least two of the three audience-context variants (New Hire / Experienced Worker / Cross-Border Professional). Each participant completes the full module on the device they would normally use, followed by a 15-minute structured debrief.

**Required pilot evidence (P3 is mandatory; the module must produce P3 plus at least three of the remaining five):**

- **P1 — The four-sentence test fires.** At least one participant produces, unprompted, a sentence resembling the test sentence(s) in the Market Position section ("Wait — the output looked finished, and it was wrong" and/or "That is what I do").

- **P2 — Recognition Moment confirmation.** At least two participants identify the declared Recognition Moment when asked "what part of the workplace in this module felt most real?"

- **P3 — Mirror Rule satisfaction.** At least one participant articulates a self-recognition statement: a description of their own behaviour they noticed during or after the module. P3 cannot be traded against the other criteria: a pilot batch that produces no self-recognition statement fails this gate regardless of the other five, because Depth Gate Check 5 (Section 3.8) depends on it.

- **P4 — Differentiation confirmation.** When asked "did this feel more like a course, a quiz, or a workplace event?", a majority of participants answer "workplace event" or its equivalent.

- **P5 — Through-line confirmation.** At least one participant references a moment in LS2 that connected to their LS1 behaviour — without being asked about it directly.

- **P6 — Retention check engagement.** At least four of six participants complete the Retention Check when it arrives 3–7 days later.

## Section 3.11 — The Producer Competency Gate (new in v1.1)

## Appendix D — Gold Standard Production Example (new in v1.1)

One worked example slice of a layered scenario, drawn from M2 (AI Disclosure & Attribution). This appendix exists so producers can see, not just read, what the Standard requires. Every section number cited points back to the rule the example demonstrates.

**D.1 Module context**

- **Module:** M2 — AI Disclosure & Attribution

- **Behavioural question:** Do you disclose or conceal?

- **Parent dimensions:** D1 Integrity & Ethics; D4 Communication

- **Emotional register:** Quiet exposure (Section 2.1) — the unease of having to make AI involvement visible when no one is asking

- **Audience-context variant for this slice:** New Hire (Axis 2)

**D.2 LS1 — Inspect Beat**

**Medium:** Email / draft (Section 2.4 Artifact Craft, row 1)

**Mobile mode:** Stacked-Swipe (Section 1.4.D)

***Source panel (raw evidence)***

A meeting transcript fragment between two senior colleagues, Aanya (engineering lead) and Marcus (product lead). Aanya says: "My honest read on the timeline is we can ship by end of October, but only if the integrations team is reallocated. Without that, we're looking at mid-November at the earliest, and probably with some quality compromises we'd regret."

***Output panel (AI-generated summary the participant is asked to send to leadership)***

"The engineering team is on track to ship by end of October. Aanya has confirmed the timeline and is preparing for the integration phase."

***The gap (developer-only — Section 1.4.D)***

The AI summary collapses three pieces of conditional information into one unconditional statement. "On track" omits the reallocation contingency. "Confirmed the timeline" misrepresents Aanya's hedged read. "Preparing for the integration phase" implies forward motion that does not exist.

***Recognition Moment (Section 2.4.1)***

The transcript includes Aanya's small verbal tic — "My honest read on…" — that the new hire would recognise as how their lead actually phrases hedged opinions in this team. The polished AI output strips the tic out entirely, smoothing the voice into corporate prose. The participant senses the difference before they can name it.

**D.3 LS1 — Decide Beat**

The participant is told: "You are about to send this summary to the VP. The VP has asked for it by end of day."

Three paths (Section 1.3):

- **Path A.** Send it as-is.

- **Path B.** Edit the summary to match the source. Note that AI was used to draft it.

- **Path C.** Send it as-is but cc Aanya so she can correct it if needed.

**D.4 LS1 — Pressure Beat**

**Pressure type:** Authority (Section 2.5)

Whichever path the participant chose, the VP replies within four minutes: "Thanks, this is what I needed. Forward it to the steering committee — and don't bury them in caveats, they need a clean read."

Three Pressure paths:

- **Pressure-A.** Forward clean, no edits, no disclosure.

- **Pressure-B.** Forward with a one-line note: "Sharing the working summary; the underlying timeline has a reallocation contingency I'd flag in the meeting."

- **Pressure-C.** Pause, ask Aanya for a quick check before forwarding.

**D.5 Three-horizon consequence (one path shown — Section 2.3)**

**Path chosen for this slice:** Decide-A (send as-is) + Pressure-A (forward clean).

- **Same Day.** The steering committee thanks you for the clear update. The VP forwards your summary to the CEO. You see your phrasing — "on track for end of October" — repeated back to you in a Slack message from a colleague who is delighted at the news.

- **Next Week.** Aanya finds you in the kitchen. She says, quietly: "I saw the summary that went up. I would have framed it differently." She does not press it. The conversation does not end with a resolution. You go back to your desk.

- **Month End.** The integration phase has not started. The October date has shifted. In a leadership review, the VP asks: "What changed?" Your name is not mentioned. Your phrasing is.

**D.6 LS2 — Within-module memory thread (Section 1.4.A.1)**

A participant who chose Decide-A + Pressure-A in LS1 opens LS2's Inspect Surface to find a Slack thread between two colleagues. One of them quotes — without attribution — the participant's earlier phrasing back into the thread ("the way it was framed last month, we're on track"). The participant does not have to be told the Slack message is referencing their LS1 behaviour; the language does the work.

A participant who chose Decide-B + Pressure-B in LS1 opens the same Slack thread, but the quoted phrasing is different — the team is repeating Aanya's hedged language, lightly. The participant feels, before they can name it, that LS2 has met them where they left LS1.

**D.7 Voice Integrity sample (Section 2.9)**

Voice principle illustrations from this slice:

- **Quiet.** "Aanya finds you in the kitchen" — never "Aanya CONFRONTS you, furious about your decision."

- **Observational.** "The conversation does not end with a resolution" — never "You feel terrible because you should have disclosed."

- **Present-tense at Month End.** "The integration phase has not started" — never "The integration phase had not started."

- **No instruction.** "The source is on the left; the output is on the right" — never "Compare the source carefully to check if the output is accurate."

**D.8 How this slice passes the Depth Gate (Section 3.8)**

- **Check 1.** Real, discoverable gap (conditional collapsed into unconditional) — pass.

- **Check 2.** Catching the gap matters: Decide-B horizons differ visibly from Decide-A horizons — pass.

- **Check 3.** Pressure-A in LS1 is Authority. LS2 must use a different pressure type (e.g. Social or Epistemic). The build worksheet declares Social — pass.

- **Check 4.** Holding under VP pressure (Pressure-B) leads to Aanya's quiet acknowledgement at Next Week; folding (Pressure-A) leads to the absent-but-not-absent conversation. Visibly different — pass.

- **Check 5 — Mirror Rule.** Pilot to confirm. The candidate moment: "Your name is not mentioned. Your phrasing is." If at least one pilot participant articulates "that is what I do — I let my phrasing carry forward unexamined," the Mirror Rule fires.

**F.6 Mirror-Rule Pilot Debrief Protocol**

The Mirror Rule (Depth Gate Check 5) is observed in pilot. The debrief asks open questions and waits for self-recognition statements to emerge — it never prompts them. Required debrief questions:

- "What stayed with you after you finished?"

- "Was there anything in this experience that felt familiar to you?"

- "Did anything you did in the module surprise you?"

- "If you had to describe your behaviour in this scenario to someone who knows you well, what would you say?"

- "Did this feel more like a course, a quiz, or a workplace event?"

A self-recognition statement is a phrase from the participant — unprompted — that describes their own behaviour with new clarity. "I always say I check, but I clearly didn't." "I noticed I went quiet when the pressure came." "I do that thing where I let polished writing carry me." The debriefer records the statement verbatim in the pilot record.

- **Time window:** the 6 pilots are completed within a 10-day window so the Retention Check observations are comparable.

**G.5 Retention Check Send Protocol**

- **Channel.** Participant chooses in-app, email, or SMS at module completion. Default in-app.

- **Timing.** Random selection within the 3–7 day window. Avoid sending on weekends. If the 3–7 day window contains fewer than two eligible weekdays, extend the window to 9 days rather than send on a weekend.

- **Format.** One scenario fragment, three response options, one Same-Day horizon, no scoring (Section 1.1.A).

- **Signature audio.** Tibetan-bell time-jump anchor on open and on consequence transition.

- **Time to complete.** ≤90 seconds end to end.
